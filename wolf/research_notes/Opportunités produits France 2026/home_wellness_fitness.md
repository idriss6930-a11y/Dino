## Q1. Which candidates show real, recent (2025–2026) French demand, and how strong is it?

### Takeaway
- **Best-documented French demand:**
  - **C6:** the air-fryer installed base keeps growing (about 2.6–2.8 M units sold in 2024; 3.4 M in 2025; 27–38% of households equipped).
  - **C8:** Amazon.fr models with about 3,900 to 10,000+ reviews, Decathlon own-brand models, and a Vinted second-hand category. Demand is large but the category is mature.
- **Strongest momentum:** C4. Pilates was ClassPass's most-booked workout in 2025 for the third year running, and Club Pilates is expanding in France. Caveat: this evidence is about studios and is global; none of it covers home accessories in France.
- **Clear, measurable French pain point:** C1. Surveys show about 68% limescale-related dissatisfaction, and Veolia reports 80% of consumers dissatisfied once water is ≥20°f. Île-de-France water is often above 30°f. The product is in mainstream retail (Leroy Merlin, Philips Water) and has fresh 2026 affiliate tests. However, I found no official national share of the population with hard water.
- **Steady, established categories:** C3 and C7.
- **Thin French-specific evidence:** C2, C5 and C9.

### Cited Findings

#### C1: Shower filter / water hardness
- **Hardness scale used by a French utility (2024):** ≤6°f very soft; 6–15°f soft; 15–30°f moderately hard; 30–40°f hard. Official per-commune results are published by the health ministry's control programme. — [Estérel Côte d'Azur Agglomération, Dec 2024](https://esterelcotedazur-agglo.fr/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Durete-eau.pdf)
- **Other thresholds:**
  - ARS Pays de la Loire calls water "calcaire" above 20°f. — [ARS Pays de la Loire](https://www.pays-de-la-loire.ars.sante.fr/media/126618/download)
  - Mes Dépanneurs classes 15–30°f as moderately hard and >30°f as very hard. — [Mes Dépanneurs](https://www.mesdepanneurs.fr/blog/durete-eau)
  - Selectra classes 8–15°f as soft. — [Selectra](https://eau.selectra.info/faq/durete-eau)
- **No national population share found.** No official national "% of population with hard water" figure appeared in any search. Selectra publishes department-average hardness (TH in °f) and a per-commune lookup built on official SISE-Eaux data (per search snippet; I could not open the page). — [Selectra – dureté de l'eau](https://eau.selectra.info/faq/durete-eau)
- **Île-de-France:** water hardness (TH) "often exceeds 30°f" (secondary source). — [lavie.bio](https://lavie.bio/?p=88549)
- **Veolia (older, 2019):** "in most French regions, water is 2 to 3 times more calcareous than it should be". From 20°f, 80% of consumers are dissatisfied with their water quality. Note this is a water-treatment company's own study. — [Veolia PDF, 2019](https://www.veolia.fr/sites/g/files/dvc2401/files/document/2019/11/934_VEOLIA_article_5-EAU-adoucissement_BAT1119.pdf)

  - The 2023 "Les Français et l'eau" barometer reports 68% limescale-linked dissatisfaction and 57% for chlorine smell/taste, and says both are rising (attribution via search snippet). — [Banque des Territoires, 2023](https://www.banquedesterritoires.fr/barometre-les-francais-et-leau-2023-les-craintes-de-penurie-lavenir-atteignent-un-nouveau-record)

### Inferences
- **Ranking by strength of evidence (not market size):** C6 ≈ C8 > C4 ≈ C1 > C3 > C7 > C9 > C2 ≈ C5.
- **C1 demand is geographically concentrated** in hard-water areas: Île-de-France >30°f, and Veolia's "most regions". Geo-targeted ads plus a postcode/commune hardness lookup would make spend efficient. Soft-water areas (Toulouse ~15.6°f is borderline) are weaker targets.
- **Central softening is a slow headwind for C1.** Utilities' decarbonation programmes (e.g., GPS&O at 55% of its population) erode the pain point locally over time.
- **US TikTok traction for C1 likely already spills into France.** Cross-border DTC brands run French-language stores: Stone Stream (fr./ch./cz. subdomains) and MineralStream (fr./ca. subdomains); see Q3.
- **Seasonality** (January peak given; the rest is inference):
  - January resolutions: C4, C5, C8.
  - November–December gifting / winter comfort: C9, C3, C8.
  - Evergreen: C1, C6.
- **Timing matters for the planner.** On 2026-11-06 there are about 7 weeks to Christmas and about 8 weeks to the January peak. Products that fit inside the existing Nuitalys store (C3, C2, C9-eye mask) can go live faster than products that need a new brand/store (C1, C4, C5, C6, C7).
- **C4's French demand is inferred from studio growth.** Home accessories are the "can't afford the studio" substitute. That logic is plausible but not directly evidenced.

## Q4. What are the red flags and regulatory flags (returns, medical claims, batteries/electrical, food contact, fragility)?

### Takeaway
- **Universal:** the GPSR (EU General Product Safety Regulation) applies to all nine. It requires an EU-responsible economic operator and manufacturer/responsible-person details on each listing.
- **Medical-claim (MDR, EU Medical Device Regulation) risk:** highest for C7 and C2. Que Choisir calls posture T-shirts useless, and competing correctors are sold as Class I medical devices.
- **Misleading-claim risk for C1:** most shower filters do not remove calcium/magnesium, and 60 Millions' journalist calls the claims "fantaisistes".
- **Electrical, battery and e-waste (DEEE) obligations:** these make C8 and C9 operationally heavier.
- **Food-contact compliance and glass breakage:** apply to C6.
- **REP (French extended producer responsibility):** the sports-and-leisure stream (ASL) clearly covers non-electric fitness gear (C4, C5).

### Cited Findings

#### Cross-cutting rules
- **GPSR basics:**
  - Regulation (EU) 2023/988 has applied directly since 13 Dec 2024. — [Koksal (legal summary)](https://koksal.av.tr/en/legislation/eu-general-product-safety-regulation-gpsr/)
  - Under Art. 16, a covered product can only be placed on the EU market if an EU-established economic operator is responsible for it.
